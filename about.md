David Allsopp’s twin existence as a [countertenor](https://web.archive.org/web/20171006013605/http://www.davidallsopp.com/) of [moderate renown](https://www.hyperion-records.co.uk/a.asp?a=A1963)
and [computer scientist](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk) began in 2000. He was a
choral scholar at [King’s College, Cambridge](https://www.kings.cam.ac.uk/kings-college-choir),
spending four years in the world-famous choir while also studying computer
science at the, um, world-famous computer laboratory.

After graduating, thinking that pursuing a career in technology was perhaps too
obvious, or perhaps not really thinking at all, he decided to fund a tiny
technology start-up on the salary of lay clerk at [Westminster Cathedral](https://westminstercathedral.org.uk/music/).
This led to a series of not-entirely-finished, but also
not-entirely-dysfunctional, computer systems, mainly written in OCaml, alongside
many recordings and concert performances all over Europe, and occasionally
further afield.

In late 2016, David joined [OCaml Labs](http://ocamllabs.io) in the [Systems Research Group](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/srg)
at Cambridge, with an initial brief to complete a Windows port of OCaml’s package manager,
OPAM, which he had prototyped on a transatlantic flight returning from a series
of performances celebrating 800 years of Magna Carta for, amongst others, the
justices of the U.S. Supreme Court. It turned out prototyping was somewhat
faster than delivering and, with a few distractions from the Multicore OCaml
project and the Dune build system (and a spinning out of OCaml Labs into
[Tarides](https://tarides.com)), it took a bit more than six months to deliver.
David made a brief return to the Computer Laboratory in 2025 as part of the
[Energy and Environment Group](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/research/eeg) and as a
Tarides Fellow. He is now a software engineer at [Jane Street](https://janestreet.com),
where he continues to get to work on the OCaml compiler.

This blog contains various bits of the technical part of David’s journey so far.
Views expressed are his own, which is to say that things which make you happy
may be associated with employers past and present; things which make you sad
should certainly not be associated with employers past or most especially
present and may be politely directed towards [@dra27.uk](https://bsky.app/profile/dra27.uk/)
or impolitely directed towards `/dev/null`.
